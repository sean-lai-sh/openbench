I've written `re.json` and it passes every test I ran. It has 408 regex/replacement pairs and is about 400 KB, well under both limits. It's built by a script, `gen.py`, and `test.py` is the harness that compares results against python-chess.

**Test results**
- **Your example:** it produces exactly the three expected positions.
- **`check.py`:** all 18 positions from the Morphy Opera Game pass. `check.py` reads from `/app/re.json`, so I ran a copy with the path changed to this directory.
- **Random games:** about 162,000 white-to-move positions from random games all matched python-chess, with no failures. The random moves were weighted towards castling, en passant and promotion so those got plenty of coverage.
- **Edge cases:** I also checked these by hand: an en-passant capture that would expose the king along the rank, castling through or out of check, castling rights lost when a rook is captured on a8/h8, and promotions with and without a capture.

**How it works**
1. **Unpack the FEN:** the board is expanded into a fixed-width line, with dots for empty squares and two separator characters after each rank so moves can't wrap from one edge to the other. Castling rights and the en-passant file go into fixed slots at the end of the line.
2. **List the possible moves:** for each square and piece type, one regex appends every possible move by that piece (knight, king, rook/queen lines, bishop/queen diagonals, pawns) as a new line. Each new line carries a small tag recording what has to be true for the move to be possible, such as the path being empty or the target square holding a black piece. Lines whose tag fails are deleted afterwards. En passant and the two castling moves have their own rules.
3. **Remove illegal moves:** one regex deletes every position where a white king is attacked. For castling, a temporary white king is placed on each square the king starts on or passes through, so a single check covers "not in check, not through check, not into check". The real king and rook positions are filled in afterwards.
4. **Update castling rights:** rights are dropped based on the new board (king not on e1, or a rook missing from a1/h1/a8/h8).
5. **Rebuild the FEN:** the output is written with side to move `b` and move counters `0 0`.

**Things to know**
- **En-passant field in the output:** after any white double pawn push, the output always names the en-passant square, even if black can't actually capture. `check.py` accepts this, because it also tries each line with that field replaced by `-`.
- **Speed:** keeping the rule count under 512 matters. Python's `re` module caches only 512 compiled patterns, so with fewer rules than that each position takes about 3 ms once the cache is warm. An earlier version with about 2,400 rules took around 0.4 s per position.
