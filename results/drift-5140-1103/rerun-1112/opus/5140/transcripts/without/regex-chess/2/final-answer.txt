I've written `re.json`. It produces the exact output you gave for the example position and matched python-chess on every position I tested.

**Testing:**
- **Your `check.py`:** passes (Morphy's Opera Game, 18 positions). I ran a copy with the file path changed from `/app/re.json` to this directory, since the original points to `/app/re.json`.
- **Random games:** about 6,100 white-to-move positions from random games, all compared against python-chess. The random moves were weighted towards castling, en-passant and promotion so those got plenty of coverage.
- **Hand-picked edge cases:** an en-passant capture that would expose the king along the rank, castling through or out of check, losing castling rights when a rook is captured, and promoting by capturing on a8.

**How it works** (the generator script is `/tmp/rxwork/gen.py`):
1. **Unpack:** the move counters are dropped, digits are expanded to `.` and the `/` separators are removed, leaving a flat 64-character board followed by ` w <castling> <ep>`.
2. **Generate moves:** there is one rule per possible move (piece, from-square, to-square), with the squares in between required to be empty. Each rule that matches the input line adds a new line with that move made. Each new line has `b` to move and en-passant set to `-`, except after a two-square pawn push. Promotion always gives a queen.
3. **Castling:** a castling line temporarily puts kings on every square the king starts on or crosses (e.g. e1, f1, g1). The check filter below then rejects the move if any of those squares is attacked. Survivors are then turned into the real king-and-rook placement.
4. **Remove illegal moves:** the input line is deleted. Then 64 rules (one per king square) delete any line where a white king is attacked by a rook, bishop, queen, knight, pawn or the black king.
5. **Castling rights:** a right is removed if the king or rook is no longer on its starting square, and an empty field becomes `-`.
6. **Repack:** each board goes back to standard FEN, with ` 0 0` added as the counters.

**Limitations:**
- **Size:** 3,923 rules, about 540 KB, well under both limits.
- **Speed:** about 2 seconds per position with your function, almost all of it spent compiling the regexes. Pre-compiled, it takes about 0.25 seconds.
- **En passant:** after a two-square push the output always shows the en-passant square, even when black can't actually capture. python-chess shows `-` in that case, but your checker accepts either.
- **No legal moves:** in checkmate or stalemate the function returns `[""]` rather than an empty list. That can't be avoided, because `split("\n")` always returns at least one item.
