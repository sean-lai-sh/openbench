I've written `re.json`: 2,043 regex/replacement pairs, about 349 KB, well under both limits. Run on the example position, it gives exactly the three expected positions.

**Testing:**
- **Provided checker:** I ran `check.py` against the file (pointed at the local path instead of `/app/re.json`). All 18 Opera Game positions pass.
- **Random games:** 2,894 white-to-move positions from 30 random games all matched python-chess. The random play was weighted towards castling, en passant and promotion so those came up often.
- **Hand-picked edge cases:** all 9 matched. They covered:
  - an en passant capture that would expose the king along the rank
  - castling out of check, through an attacked square, or onto one
  - a black rook blocking b1 for queenside castling
  - promoting with a capture on a8/h8, which removes Black's castling right
  - a pinned en passant capture

**How it works:**
1. **Parse:** the FEN becomes a fixed-width line: the 8×8 board (empty squares as `.`, ranks separated by `/`), then the castling rights as 4 fixed slots (`-` when a right is gone), then the en passant square.
2. **Generate candidate moves:** there is one regex for each from-square/to-square pair for pawns (push, double push, captures, en passant, queen-only promotion), knights, sliding pieces and the king. When a move is possible, the regex copies the line with the move applied, updates castling rights based on the squares involved, and sets the en passant square after a double push.
3. **Castling:** the candidate line temporarily puts extra kings on the squares the king passes through. That way the next step rejects castling out of, through, or into check.
4. **Remove illegal moves:** one regex per square deletes any line where a white king on that square is attacked.
5. **Format output:** the remaining lines are turned back into FENs with `b` to move.

**Two limitations:**
- **Speed:** it takes about 2 seconds per position under the given runner (about 35 s for the whole `check.py` run). That's because Python only caches 512 compiled regexes, so all 2,043 get recompiled on every call.
