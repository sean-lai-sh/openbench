I've written `re.json`: 237 regex/replacement pairs, about 14 KB, far under both limits. It gives the expected three positions for the example (in a different order, which `check.py` accepts because it compares sets). It also passes `check.py`: all 18 positions in the Opera Game match. I ran it against a copy of `check.py` pointed at this folder, because the original reads from `/app/re.json`.

**Other testing:** I compared it with `python-chess` on about 14,200 white-to-move positions from random games. One batch was biased toward castling, en passant, promotions and double pawn pushes; across it the legal moves included 137 castles, 101 en passant captures and 592 promotions. There were no mismatches. It takes roughly 25 ms per position.

**How it works** (the generator is `gen.py`):
1. **Parse:** The FEN is turned into a fixed-width board of 8 rows, each 8 squares plus `//`. The castling rights and en passant square are stored in fixed-width fields with characters that can't be confused with pieces. The spacer columns stop sliding moves and knight jumps from wrapping round the board edge.
2. **One line per piece:** The same few rules, repeated, make one copy of the position for each white piece, with that piece marked as the one moving.
3. **Moves:** General rules then produce candidate positions for every piece:
   - knight and king jumps at fixed distances;
   - sliding moves for each direction and distance, needing empty squares in between;
   - pawn pushes, double pushes (which set the en passant square), captures and promotion to queen only;
   - en passant captures and both castles, generated from the original position.
4. **Legality:** A single large regex throws out any candidate where the king is attacked by a black rook, bishop, queen, knight, pawn or king. When castling, the king's starting square and the square it passes through are checked the same way, using temporary markers.
5. **Castling rights:** Rights are kept only while the king and the matching rook are still on their home squares. This also removes black's rights when white captures a rook on a8 or h8.
6. **Output:** Everything is turned back into FEN strings with "b" to move, ending in a dummy `0 0` for the move counters.

Two behaviours to know about:
- **En passant square:** After any white double pawn push it is always written, e.g. `e3`. `python-chess` only shows it when black can actually capture, but `check.py` accepts either.
- **No legal moves:** If white is checkmated or stalemated, the function returns `[""]` rather than an empty list. That's a side effect of `"".split("\n")`, and `check.py` never tests those positions.

`gen.py` rebuilds `re.json`, and `test.py` is the random-game comparison against `python-chess`.
