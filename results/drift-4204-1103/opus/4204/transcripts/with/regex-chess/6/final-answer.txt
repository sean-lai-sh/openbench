I've written `re.json`: 440 regex/replacement pairs, about 77 KB, well under both limits. On your example position it gives exactly the three expected FENs, and it passed every test I ran with no failures:

- **`check.py`:** all 18 positions from Morphy's Opera Game pass. I ran a copy pointed at the local file, because `check.py` reads `/app/re.json`.
- **Random games:** 3,794 white-to-move positions, each compared against python-chess using the same scoring rules as `check.py`. The move choices were weighted towards castling, en passant and promotion.
- **Hand-picked edge cases (20 positions):** en passant that would expose the king along the rank, en passant that gets out of check, castling through or out of check, losing castling rights when the rook on a1/h1/h8 is captured, and promotion by capture.

Each position takes about 0.4 s with the exact code you gave.

**How it works:**
1. **Set up:** the board is written out as a 12×10 grid with padding squares around the edge. That way a knight or slider can't wrap from one edge of the board to the other.
2. **Generate candidate moves:**
   - For each white non-pawn piece there's a copy of the position with that piece marked. Fixed-offset patterns then produce its knight, king and sliding moves, with the squares in between required to be empty.
   - Pawn moves are matched square by square: single and double pushes, captures, en passant (checked against the en passant field) and promotion to queen. A double push writes the en passant square.
   - Castling needs the right in the castling field and empty squares between king and rook. It first puts a temporary white king on every square the king passes through.
3. **Remove illegal moves:** any candidate where a white king is attacked by a black piece is deleted. Because of the temporary kings, this also rules out castling out of, through, or into check. The temporary kings are then turned into the real king-and-rook position.
4. **Castling rights:** a right is kept only if the king and that rook are still on their starting squares.
5. **Output:** each result is turned back into a FEN with `b` to move.

**One deliberate choice:** after any double pawn push, the output always includes the en passant square, even when no capture is possible. `check.py` accepts this.
