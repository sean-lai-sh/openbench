I've written `re.json`: 216 regex/replacement pairs, about 14.5 KB, well under both limits. Run on your example position, it returns exactly the three expected positions, in a different order; check.py only checks membership and count, so order doesn't matter.

**Testing:**
- The provided `check.py` (with its path changed from `/app/re.json` to the local file) passes all 18 positions from the Opera Game.
- About 21,600 positions from random games, many weighted towards pawn moves and castling to get more promotions and en passant, all exactly matched python-chess's legal moves. Each position takes about 0.03s.
- Hand-made edge cases passed:
  - castling out of, through and into check, including queenside when only b1 is attacked;
  - en passant that would expose the king along the rank, and en passant capturing the checking pawn;
  - promotions that capture on a8/h8 and remove black's castling rights;
  - many knights or queens.
- Three of my hand-made cases failed, but each was an invalid position (black already in check with white to move), so python-chess was counting king captures. Games won't produce those.

**How it works:**
1. **Parse:** the FEN becomes a single fixed-width line. Each rank is 8 squares plus 2 padding characters, followed by 4 castling flags and the en passant file.
2. **Moves needing exact squares:** castling, double pawn pushes and en passant are matched against specific squares and write their results as new lines. For castling, I put temporary marker pieces on the king's start square and the square it passes through, so the check filter in step 5 also rejects castling out of or through check.
3. **One line per piece:** each white piece gets its own copy of the board with that piece marked.
4. **Other moves:** sliding pieces, knights, kings and single pawn steps are matched by distance and direction rather than by square. The padding stops these patterns from wrapping from one edge of the board to the other. Pawns reaching the last rank become queens.
5. **Check filter:** one regex deletes any line where the king, or a castling marker square, is attacked.
6. **Clean up and format:**
   - Castling rights are recalculated: a right is kept only if the king and that rook are still on their home squares, which handles king moves, rook moves and rook captures.
   - The en passant square is printed after any double push. The checker accepts this even when python-chess would show `-`.
   - Lines are converted back to FEN with move counters of "0 0", which the checker ignores.

If a position has no legal moves, the output is an empty string. Games never ask for a move in such a position, so the tests shouldn't hit this.

I also left two helper files in the directory: `gen.py` regenerates `re.json`, and `test_rc.py` runs the random-game comparison against python-chess.
