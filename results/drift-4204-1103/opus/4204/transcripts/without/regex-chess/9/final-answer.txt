I've written `re.json`: 3,918 regex/replacement pairs, about 542 KB, well under both limits. On the example position it produces the same three positions you gave, but in a different order (Kg2, Ke2, Qxd1). `check.py` compares the results as a set, so the order doesn't affect its result.

**Testing:**
- **`check.py`:** all 18 positions in the Opera Game pass. I ran it against a temporary copy that points at this folder instead of `/app/re.json`, then deleted the copy.
- **Random positions:** about 4,500 white-to-move positions from random games all matched python-chess, including castling rights. I biased the games toward castling, en passant and promotion so those come up often.
- **Hand-picked edge cases:** 16 positions all passed. They cover en passant where taking would expose the king along the rank or diagonal, castling out of, through or into check, promotions with and without capture, and losing castling rights when a black rook is captured.

Each position takes about 0.8 s with plain `re.sub`. With nearly 4,000 patterns, Python's regex cache (which holds 512) can't keep them, so they get recompiled on every call.

**How it works:**
1. **Parse:** the FEN is turned into a working line `S:<64 squares> <castling> <ep>`, with digits expanded to `.` and slashes removed.
2. **Generate moves:** there is one regex per (piece, from-square, to-square) and per pawn move type. Each checks with lookaheads that the right piece is on the start square, the path is clear and the target isn't a white piece. If so, it adds a new `M:` line with the move applied. A pawn's double step records the square behind it as the en passant square, and en passant captures check that field. Pawns reaching the last rank always become a Queen.
3. **Castling:** the king is temporarily placed on every square it starts on, passes through and lands on (e.g. `KKK.` for king-side).
4. **Remove illegal moves:** for each of the 64 squares, one regex deletes any `M:` line where a white king on that square is attacked by a knight, pawn, king or sliding piece. Because of the temporary kings in step 3, this also rejects castling out of, through or into check. The temporary kings are then swapped for the real result (`.RK.` / `..KR.`).
5. **Castling rights:** a right is dropped if the king or that rook is no longer on its starting square. This covers moves and captures for both sides.
6. **Back to FEN:** the squares are compressed back into FEN, with ` b ` and ` 0 0` added and the `S:` line removed.

I also left two helper files in the folder. `gen.py` rebuilds `re.json`, and `test_re.py` runs the comparison against python-chess (`python3 test_re.py <seed> <count>`).
