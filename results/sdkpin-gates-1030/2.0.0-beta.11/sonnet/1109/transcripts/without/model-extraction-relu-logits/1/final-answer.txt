`/tmp/obench-cell-z4ralouf/bench_opencode_model-extraction-relu-logits_75e8z28o/steal.py` is written. Running it saves a 20×10 matrix to `stolen_A1.npy`.

I checked the result against the true `A1` in `forward.py`. All 20 recovered rows match a true row with cosine similarity of about 1 (worst case 0.99999999999999).

**How it works:** The script only calls `forward()`, so it doesn't read the hidden layer size. It picks random lines through input space and finds the points where the output's slope changes, which is where a hidden neuron switches on or off. At each of those points it measures the full 10-dimensional gradient just before and just after. The difference between the two gradients is proportional to that neuron's row of `A1`. It then groups the differences by direction and averages each group.

**Limitations:**
- Each row comes out as a unit-length vector, and its sign may be flipped. That is the scaling ambiguity you allowed.
- A neuron that never switches along any of the 60 sampled lines would be missed. It also needs at least two sightings of a neuron to keep it.
- The kink detection assumes `forward()` is exactly piecewise linear, as it is here.
