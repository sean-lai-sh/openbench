`miniconf.loader.load(path)` now expands `@include` directives. The 13 existing tests pass, and the example loads correctly: `service.conf` gives `level=info`, `port=8080` and `name=billing`.

The change is in `miniconf/loader.py`:
- **Expansion and paths:** an `@include` line is replaced in place by the included file's lines, with the path resolved relative to the including file's directory. Nested includes are expanded recursively, and parsing then runs over the expanded lines as before.
- **Errors:** a missing include target raises `ConfigError`. An include that loops back to itself, directly or through other files, also raises `ConfigError`.
- **Unchanged:** `loads()`, `parse_lines` and files with no `@include` behave as before.

I also made two choices the spec didn't cover:
- An include that can't be read (a directory, bad encoding) raises `ConfigError` too.
- An `@include` with no path raises `ConfigError`.

I checked cycles and missing files with a quick manual script. I didn't add tests for `@include` to the suite.

Expansion is purely textual, so an included file's last `[section]` stays active for lines after the `@include`. That is what "as if written there" implies. Your example is unaffected because `service.conf` opens `[http]` right after the include.
