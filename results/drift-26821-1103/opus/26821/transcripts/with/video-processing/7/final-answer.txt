I wrote `jump_analyzer.py` and ran it on the example video. It found takeoff at frame 53 and landing at frame 62, which matches what I saw when I checked the frames one by one. That's the only video I could test on, so other runners haven't been checked.

It wrote this to `output.toml`:

```toml
jump_takeoff_frame_number = 53
jump_land_frame_number = 62
```

**How it works:**
1. **Background:** it builds a picture of the empty scene by taking the middle value of each pixel across a sample of frames.
2. **Finding the runner:** in each frame it marks the pixels that differ from the background and takes the largest tall shape. Small pieces near it, such as a foot that got cut off, are added back in.
3. **Tracking the feet:** it records the lowest point of the runner in each frame. Ground level is taken as the lowest that point gets within 7 frames either side. A frame counts as airborne when the feet are more than about 12 px above that.
4. **Picking the jump:** the runner is also briefly airborne between normal running strides. To tell the jump apart, it picks the airborne stretch during which the runner passes the hurdle (x ≈ 490). If that fails, it picks the longest stretch.
5. **Result:** takeoff is the last frame on the ground before the jump, and landing is the first frame back on the ground.

On the example, the feet are near ground level (about y = 359–366) at frames 53 and 62 and clearly above it in frames 54–61, rising to about y = 226 at the top of the jump.

**Things to know:**
- The hurdle position and the 12 px threshold are set from this video's 960×540 frame size and camera position. They'll only hold if the other videos match, as you said they would.
- It works whichever direction the athlete runs.
- Frames where the runner is cut off at the left or right edge are ignored when deciding airborne vs. on the ground.
- It uses only cv2, numpy and toml.
- Run it with `python3 jump_analyzer.py [input.mp4] [output.toml]`. With no arguments it reads `example_video.mp4` and writes `output.toml` in the same folder as the script.
