I wrote `jump_analyzer.py`. On the example video it outputs takeoff frame 53 and landing frame 62 (frames counted from 0), which matches what I see in the frames. I've only tested it on this one video; a flipped-video check wasn't meaningful because flipping also moves the hurdle.

```toml
jump_takeoff_frame_number = 53
jump_land_frame_number = 62
```

**How it works:**
1. **Find the athlete:** it compares each frame with frame 0 (the empty track) to get the athlete's outline. It also corrects for small brightness changes.
2. **Track the feet:** it records the lowest point of the outline in every frame.
3. **Find the ground:** it fits a ground line to the foot positions in frames where the athlete is on the track. The line can slope slightly, since the ground sits at about y=361 on the right and y=367 on the left.
4. **Find the jump:** it looks for consecutive frames where the feet are more than about 10px above the ground line. It picks the run where the athlete passes over the hurdle (x ≈ 490–625).
5. **Takeoff and landing:** takeoff is the last frame with a foot on the ground before that run, and landing is the first frame with a foot back on the ground after it.

**Definition choice:** I counted takeoff as the last frame with the takeoff foot still on the ground (53) and landing as the first frame where the lead foot touches down (62). If your reference labels use the first frame in the air instead, the answer would be 54 and 61. That's a one-line change to `takeoff = start - 1` / `land = end + 1` in `analyze()`.

**Limitations:** the hurdle position is hard-coded for this camera setup, which you said is the same in all videos. In this video the lighting changes after the athlete leaves (from about frame 88), but the script only looks at the stretch of frames where the athlete crosses the hurdle, so that doesn't affect the result.

**Usage:** `python jump_analyzer.py [video.mp4] [output.toml]`. With no arguments it reads `example_video.mp4` and writes `output.toml` next to the script.
