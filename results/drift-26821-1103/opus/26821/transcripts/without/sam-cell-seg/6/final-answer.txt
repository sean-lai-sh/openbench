I've written `convert_masks.py` in the workspace. On the demo it converts all 48 masks (19 rectangles and 29 polylines) to polylines, running on CPU in about 12 seconds. A check script confirmed that no two masks share any pixel, each mask is one connected piece, none is a rectangle, and the bounding boxes match the new coordinates. I also looked over the masks drawn on the image, and they follow the cell outlines well.

**How it works**
- **Model:** It loads MobileSAM (`vit_t`) from `weights_path`. The MobileSAM source is untouched.
- **Prompts:** Each cell's box goes to MobileSAM along with a point inside the cell. For existing polylines, the hand-drawn outline is also given as a hint.
- **Picking a mask:** MobileSAM gives several candidate masks per cell. The script picks one using MobileSAM's own confidence score, how well it matches the original annotation, and how much of it lies inside the box. Masks that are much too big or too small for the box are rejected.
- **Clean-up:**
  - Thin spurs are trimmed, holes are filled, and only one connected piece is kept per cell.
  - Where two masks overlap, each pixel goes to the cell MobileSAM is more confident about.
  - If a cell ends up with almost nothing, it falls back to its own annotation or, as a last resort, a tiny shape at the box centre, so every row still gets a polygon.
- **Large images:** Images bigger than 1024 px are processed in overlapping 1024 px tiles, so small cells keep full resolution. I tested this on a 4×4 tiled copy of the demo (768 cells, about 1m50s): no overlaps and no failures.
- **Output:** The CSV keeps the same rows, columns and index column. Only `xmin`, `ymin`, `xmax`, `ymax`, `coords_x` and `coords_y` are updated, with each polyline closed like the input (first point repeated at the end).
